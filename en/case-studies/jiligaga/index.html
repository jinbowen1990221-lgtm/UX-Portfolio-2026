ANCHOR CASE · SENIOR UX / OVERSEAS EDTECH

Rebuilding the learning starting point,so children want to come back

Jiligaga serves children aged 2–8 overseas. I reframed onboarding from a registration step into preparation for the first lesson, then tracked sign-up, lesson entry, completion, and D3 / D7 returns from V1.5 onward. Every iteration answered the same question: why would a child want to keep learning?

01 / CONTEXT & TENSION

Three barriers had to be removed before children could enter their first lesson

Parents needed to understand why information was collected, children needed to feel the course value quickly, and the early-stage business needed more families to reach a first lesson.

USER TENSION

Why collect information about a child before learning begins?

Age and English proficiency are collected to match each child with suitable content and recommend the right course level.

BUSINESS TENSION

The business needed more first lessons, without copying a mature product’s payment funnel.

Jiligaga was still in an early market-expansion stage. Getting families into a lesson and helping them understand the product mattered more than charging them first.

DESIGN TENSION

How might we set expectations first, then collect only the information we need?

The real problem was not removing a few screens. It was creating continuity from entry point and data purpose to level recommendation and first-lesson value.

02 / RESEARCH → DECISION

Research did not reveal one standard flow. It helped us decide what needed to happen first.

Evidence came from four Taiwanese family interviews, competitor onboarding research, and funnels from V1.6–V1.10. Together they pointed to one move: explain value and data purpose before sending users into the first lesson.

Evidence boundary

The interview sample included four families. Version data is a directional comparison with concurrent variables; it locates problems and guides iteration, but does not establish strict causality.

01

Children engage more with interaction; parents care more about learning feedback

Across four Taiwanese families, children responded more positively to game interactions than videos, while parents valued teaching-intensive activities such as writing and speaking.

Therefore:Put course value into learning activities that are interactive and responsive.
02

Course difficulty depends on a child's existing English proficiency

Beginners found the content appropriate, while experienced learners found the same content too easy.

Therefore:Combine age and English proficiency to recommend a level, then explain the rationale and learning outcomes.
03

Competitor patterns cannot simply be copied into an early-stage market

Competitor onboarding averaged four steps and about 1 minute 45 seconds, but Jiligaga was still acquiring its earliest users under a different business model.

Therefore:Do not copy a pay-first funnel; shorten the route to a lesson and communicate course value earlier.
04

The age screen still needed clearer expectations

Version reviews still identified drop-off on the age screen and missing progress and data-purpose explanations. This page-level observation is separate from the aggregate version ratios below.

Therefore:Explain the purpose before asking for age and set clear expectations for the flow.

ONBOARDING ANALYSIS · V1.6 → V1.10

Reframe onboarding from an entry-point contest into the first step of course recommendation.

The Have an Account click rate rose from 4% to 15%. Combined with roughly 10% loss at the start of sign-up, we treated this as a routing signal rather than a growth result. V1.10 therefore clarified the new-user path, explained data purpose, and recommended a level directly by age.

04 / EVIDENCE → DESIGN QUESTIONS

What the numbers show, and what still needs testing.

01 / TRAFFIC

Check the traffic mix

Visits rose from 347,892 to 913,857. Review channel and audience mix before comparing efficiency.

02 / SIGN-UP

Clarify the starting path

Visit-to-sign-up fell 7.01 pp. Review the entry promise and sign-up friction.

03 / LESSON ENTRY

Explain course recommendations

Sign-up-to-lesson entry fell 4.72 pp. Investigate recommendation clarity and the next action.

04 / FIRST LESSON

Review the first-lesson experience

Lesson-entry-to-completion fell 13.89 pp. Check difficulty, interruptions and completion feedback.

05 / D3 RETURN

Validate the return rhythm

D3 / first completion rose 12.88 pp. Compare matched cohorts to investigate the contribution of reminders and tasks.

06 / D7 RETURN

Check sustained learning

D7 / first completion rose 7.46 pp. Validate the observation window and continued learning behavior.

These are observations and testable design directions, not weighted opportunity shares. Positive and negative changes are reported together; aggregate comparisons do not isolate a single design effect.

06 / PRODUCT EXPRESSION

01 / 02

Part 1 · Visual and information upgrade

Rebuild the course home as a learning map children can understand.

This was not a reskin. It reworked product positioning, information hierarchy, and the route into a course.

0–1 incubationProduct architectureVisual localizationConversion path
→
01

Product architecture redesign

Using Lingokids, Khan Kids, and local family profiles as references, we repositioned the product from online courses to an educational digital toy, with a horizontal level map and full-screen interaction standards.

02

Visual localization

Visual research in Taiwan showed that the original high-saturation style did not build local trust, so the system shifted to pastel green, rounded type, and friendlier character language.

03

Conversion loop

Connect off-site ads, in-app claims, LINE retention, and subscription paths, while using skeleton states to reduce abandonment during loading.

06 / PRODUCT EXPRESSION

02 / 02

Part 2 · Learning rhythm and return motivation

Extend one completed lesson into a sustainable seven-day learning rhythm.

After the first lesson, generate a weekly mission so daily progress, streak challenges, and completion rewards lead into the next visit.

Seven-day check-inImmediate feedbackStreak challengeReturn trigger
→
01

Make the weekly rhythm visible

A seven-day calendar, Today state, and weekly progress show children and parents exactly where they are.

02

Respond immediately to completion

Medals, ribbons, and a completed state acknowledge effort and turn abstract progress into a tangible reward.

03

Leave a goal for the next visit

Visible streak weeks and medal totals keep the next goal in view and create a clear trigger for D3 / D7 returns.
